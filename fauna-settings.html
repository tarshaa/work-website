+ Product design · Agent behavior

Fauna — designing agent behavior.

A creative assistant needs to communicate more than an answer. This study explores how Fauna receives a brief, shows work in progress, and returns a result that people can review and shape.

A clear thread through the task.

The request stays in view while the assistant changes state. A consistent central panel anchors the interaction; motion and status text explain the transition without displacing the work.

The animation explores three distinct moments: expressing intent, understanding progress, and reviewing the next step. The annotations call out the controls and feedback that make each moment legible.

+ Prompt

Start with intent.

The prompt establishes what the creator wants to make. Suggestions give the conversation a starting point, while context and references help ground the request.

Design focus: an approachable starting state with a clear primary action.

+ Thinking

Make progress understandable.

The original request remains visible as Fauna works. A named activity and animated indicator communicate what is happening, and the follow-up field keeps steering close to the task.

Design focus: useful status feedback without making the creator leave the conversation.

+ Output

Give the result a next step.

The output becomes something to inspect and develop. A structured draft brings the plan into view, with an explicit action for continuing into a storyboard.

Design focus: a reviewable result and a clear handoff back to the creator.

+ Pattern variations

One identity, several ways to move.

Six variations explore how the same form can flow, breathe, reflect, gather, turn, and reverse. Seeing them together makes their timing and character easier to compare.

Swipe to compare the variations.

+ Motion language

An identity that stays recognizable in motion.

A closer look at the animated Fauna mark. Its restrained movement gives the assistant a recognizable presence alongside the functional state changes.
